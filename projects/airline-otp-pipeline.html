01 Overview

A data-engineering pipeline built at a size where the engineering decisions actually matter: 72,961,836 US flight records — every month from January 2015 to the present, ~30 GB of raw CSV from the Bureau of Transportation Statistics — cleaned, warehoused, tested, orchestrated, and delivered to a dashboard.

The distinguishing property is that it stays current on its own: a monthly Airflow DAG whose first scheduled run ingested a new month of data end to end with nobody watching. Everything below was measured over the full history, not a sample.

The layering is deliberate: Spark does structural cleaning only — types, dedup, null-key filtering. All business logic (KPI definitions, lookups) lives in dbt where it is SQL-reviewable, tested, and documented.

Ingestion that a scheduler can trust

An idempotent downloader with atomic writes (.tmp + rename) and retry with backoff. A re-running scheduler must never let Spark read a partially written CSV — so a partial file is never visible under its final name.

2

Spark cleaning, explicitly typed

CSVs are read all-string and cast explicitly. Schema inference double-scans 26 GB and can guess differently across 120 monthly files; explicit casts fail loudly and survive year-over-year schema drift. Natural-key dedup, flag normalization, null-key filtering — structural work only.

04 Decisions worth defending

Read all-string, cast explicitly

Inference costs a second full scan of 26 GB and can disagree between monthly files. Explicit casts fail loudly at the boundary and survive schema drift across a decade of BTS releases.

Spark cleans, dbt decides

Types, dedup, and null keys in Spark. Every business definition in dbt, where it is SQL, reviewable, tested, and documented — instead of a transformation nobody can audit.

Partitioned by year/month

Matches BTS's own monthly grain, which is what makes incremental warehouse loads and predicate pushdown possible rather than reprocessing the decade.

HHMM times left as strings

Turning wall-clock times into timestamps needs timezone context. Deferred to dbt rather than done half-correctly in Spark and silently wrong at DST boundaries.

local[*] on purpose

The dataset exceeds laptop RAM but not laptop disk. The identical job runs on Databricks or EMR by swapping the master — so the cluster is a deployment choice, not a prerequisite.

Booleans typed at the edge

BTS ships "1.00"/"0.00" flags; empty flags become NULL (unknown), not false — because an unknown cancellation silently read as "not cancelled" corrupts every downstream rate.
