Demand Forecasting with Cost-Aware A/B Testing

The full Walmart M5 panel — all 30,490 store-SKU series — carried past the leaderboard to the question a real team has to answer: does the better model actually save money, and should it ship? A pre-registered A/B test on a newsvendor cost simulation says yes — SHIP at −14.1%. The whole pipeline runs on a 16 GB laptop in ~20 minutes.

01 Overview

M5 is usually a Kaggle accuracy contest. This project treats accuracy as the start, not the finish: a lower error metric doesn't matter unless it changes an ordering decision and saves money. So every forecast is turned into an order quantity, every ordering mistake is priced (understock costs 5× overstock), and the two systems are compared as a pre-registered A/B test.

What makes it credible is the discipline: the decision rule is written down before results, model selection and confirmation happen on separate time windows, and the honest failures — including two earlier HOLD verdicts — are preserved in the git history rather than edited out.

Chunked ETL to weekly grain

Melting all series at once needs 20–30 GB. Instead, per-store chunks (10 × ~3,049 series) build weekly features without ever materialising the 59M-row daily frame — 68s, 3.7 GB peak, sums reconcile exactly. Daily is 62% zero-sales noise; weekly cuts that to ~12% volume-weighted.

Decision-driving EDA

LightGBM + classical challenger

One LightGBM per store (Tweedie objective for zero-inflated demand, recursive multi-step) vs AutoETS (season 52) as the classical challenger it must beat — and does. A 50/50 blend of the two is the accuracy champion; each family fixes the other's errors.

4

Newsvendor cost simulation

Every forecast becomes an order; the newsvendor-optimal order is the τ* ≈ 0.833 demand quantile (5:1 understock:overstock). The treatment model learns that quantile per SKU with pinball loss instead of "mean + z·σ" — same target, better estimator.

newsvendorpinball loss
5

Pre-registered A/B test

The decision rule is fixed before results. τ is selected on folds 1–2 and judged once on fold 3 (chosen τ = 0.866). A paired design over ~30K series, with the ≥5% practical-significance gate — not the p-value — carrying the decision.

Verdict & sensitivity

SHIP at −14.1%, holding at 3:1 economics; against the same safety-stock policy on LightGBM-mean, quantile ordering still saves −9.4% — proving the ordering policy, not just the model, drives the gain.

04 What makes it rigorous

Money, not error

Every forecast becomes a priced order (understock 5:1 overstock), so the metric that decides shipping is dollars saved — not a WMAPE nobody can act on.

Pre-registered & separated

The decision rule was stated before results; τ selection (folds 1–2) and confirmation (fold 3) are kept apart, so the effect size — not a hackable p-value — carries the call.

No leakage, tested

Time-only splits; every lag/rolling feature is .shift()-ed. A unit test perturbs the current week and fails if any feature moves — 18 tests run in CI.

Full M5 on 16 GB

Per-store chunked ETL, aggressive dtypes, and per-store checkpointing keep peak RSS at 4.5 GB — any crash resumes instead of restarting.

Learn the quantile, not the mean

Rather than post-hoc safety stock, the treatment predicts the cost-optimal demand quantile as the order quantity — a better estimator of the exact thing that's being decided.

Honest development trail

The first version of this test was a HOLD — the accurate model lost money by under-ordering. That arc, including both earlier HOLDs, is preserved in git rather than hidden.
