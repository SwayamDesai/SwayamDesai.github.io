01 Overview

A bank, lender, or insurer that receives a consumer complaint is on a regulatory clock. CFPB rules give 15 days to respond — and underneath that sit separate, shorter, non-interchangeable statutory clocks: Regulation E gives 10 business days to investigate an unauthorised electronic transfer, Regulation Z gives two billing cycles for a billing error, Regulation X gives 5 and 30 days for a notice of error. Missing one is a per-violation penalty, not a judgement call.

Arbiter reads a complaint, finds the regulation that governs it, drafts a cited response, checks its own claims against the cited text, and decides whether a person must look at it. A person always keeps the send button.

It is built on real public data — 8,240 CFPB consumer complaints and 3,827 chunks of Code of Federal Regulations text across seven CFR parts. Nothing is synthetic, and the split is temporal (train ≤ 2025-06, dev through 2025-12, test 2026-01 onward), so the system is always predicting forward as it would in production.

The honest read, and it is a negative result. On product the language model is probably a little better and it is not proven — the interval includes zero (+0.043 [−0.003, +0.087]). On issue, TF-IDF wins significantly (−0.056 [−0.099, −0.025]). So the conclusion is to classify with TF-IDF: microseconds, zero tokens, better on the harder head.

The language model earns its cost elsewhere — grounded drafting, citation, claim-level verification, escalation reasoning — none of which a linear model can do at all. That outcome was committed to in advance in an ADR; reporting it is the point of having baselines.

Deadline-aware intake

Each complaint is placed on the statutory clock that actually governs it, not just the 15-day CFPB response window — so the queue is ordered by real exposure.

Classification, chosen on evidence

Product and issue are predicted by TF-IDF because the measurement said so. A second, differently-biased retriever raised candidate recall from 0.64 to 0.892 on dev and 0.879 on test, moving the bottleneck off retrieval entirely.

TF-IDF + lineardual retriever~80 issue categories
3

Grounded drafting

The response is drafted against retrieved CFR text and cited to the provision. Claims are then verified at claim level against the cited passage, so a draft that over-asserts is caught before a human ever reads it.

hybrid retrievalcross-encoder rerankclaim verification
4

Deterministic safety layer

22 rules escalate the cases a generalist must not handle — bankruptcy disclosure, fair-lending allegation, litigation threat, someone in crisis. arbiter policy-audit runs every rule over every non-test narrative and fails CI if a mandatory rule exceeds its declared firing ceiling.

Calibrated automation

Self-reported model confidence is badly calibrated (ECE 0.178), so the escalation gate reads a score built from retrieval-side features instead. The operating point comes off a published risk-coverage curve rather than a default.

04 What measurement caught

Four detector bugs the prose called caution

Auditing the safety rules — instead of describing them — found four real defects, including a money pattern that read $12500 as $125, and a mandatory rule firing on "the representative told me to wait".

A negative result kept in the README

TF-IDF beats the LLM on issue classification. The repo says so, in the same table as the win, because a baseline you discard when it wins is not a baseline.

Falsifiable time savings

Edit distance between draft and sent response is recorded on every human action — a draft rewritten from scratch saved nothing, and the metric can say so.

Claims bounded by the ceiling

Every automation number is capped by the measured 61.5% eligibility. The claim is not "61.5% automated"; it is that those cases become an approval instead of an authoring task.

Stated limits

Uncalibrated confidence, a single-annotator gold retrieval set, a partial on-premise story, and an unset injection threshold are all listed in the README rather than left for a reader to discover.

Forward-only evaluation

Temporal split throughout — train on the past, evaluate on later months, so no result depends on information the system would not have had.
