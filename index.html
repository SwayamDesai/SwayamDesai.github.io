About

Careful analysis, engineering that follows through.

A model that does well in validation is only the start. The questions I find more interesting come after it: does this hold on data it hasn't seen, does it change a decision anyone is actually making, and is it worth what it costs to run?

That shows up throughout my work. A pre-registered A/B test decides whether a forecasting model is worth shipping — and said no twice before it said yes. A semantic cache and a reranker make a clinical retrieval system fast and accurate enough to trust. Evidence-backed completion gates stop an agent from claiming success without an observed patch and containerized tests that passed.

I also report what didn't work. One project retracts its own earlier retrieval finding once the ablation was run properly — the corrected result is indistinguishable from zero, and it's in the README next to the wins. Another spent two full iterations on a forecasting model that was more accurate and still lost money before the third one earned its launch. A result you'd bury when it disagrees with you isn't a measurement.

I'm comfortable across the whole workflow — framing the problem, moving the data with Spark, dbt and Airflow, building and tuning the model, wiring the serving layer, and setting up the monitoring that catches drift — on AWS, GCP and Azure.
